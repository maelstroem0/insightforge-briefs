The Week Buybacks Lost to the Bond

This week belonged to the debasement trade, and the defining fact is that official demand lost. Treasury doubled long-end buybacks on Tuesday to cap the 30-year at 5.216%; by Wednesday it had doubled them again to $4 billion and the 30-year hit 5.34% anyway. The market read the suppression for what it was — Druckenmiller called it dangerous — and repriced every scarce asset in sympathy: gold at $4,677.19, bitcoin through $80,000 on a record $1.92 billion ETF inflow, wheat up 35% in 2026. Only Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole debut on Thursday pulled the 30-year back to 5.173%, and even that breathing room is hostage to Trump's attempt to fire governor Lisa Cook. If a lower bar for removing Fed officials holds, the independence premium comes out of the long end next week — and everything leveraged to it goes with it.
