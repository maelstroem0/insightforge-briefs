Lead Story

The sell-off is global. Japan's ten-year yield hit a three-decade high. German thirty-year bund yields reached their highest since 2011, and France's thirty-year bond reached its highest since 2008. The long end is doing the repricing.

The fiscal math is the transmission channel. Debt service costs are one-point-two trillion dollars this year. Economists at Charles Schwab say Washington keeps rolling debt at market prices, so the effective rate on the entire debt stock slowly climbs.

Auction demand is strained. This week's U.S. sales cleared at yields that were the highest since 2007 and 2001, and Wall Street sees no near-term reversal in the supply calendar.

That is bearish for long-duration Treasuries, utilities, and housing. It tightens financial conditions through hawkish Fed repricing, and it pressures risk assets from credit to tech as the discount rate rises. This is the global fiscal risk premium being repriced across sovereign curves.

The next catalyst is today's Federal Reserve minutes. If policymakers signal that oil-driven inflation risks and the fiscal deficit limit rate cuts, the thirty-year could retest five-point-three-three and then push toward five and a half.

So the number to remember is five-point-three-three percent. That is the thirty-year Treasury's nineteen-year high. It has since eased to around five-point-two-seven, but the fiscal supply issue is not reversing, and the risk is that five-point-three-three becomes a floor.

Turning to the Strait of Hormuz. Iran has intensified attacks on vessels, killing one crew member, and daily transits have collapsed to between three and six ships from roughly one hundred ten.

That is about a ninety-five percent drop in traffic through the world's most important oil chokepoint. Weekly transits fell nineteen and a half percent to ninety-five, and one crew member is dead.

That is the transmission into markets. A prolonged Hormuz closure pushes insurance costs higher, lifts tanker rates and crude prices, and is bearish for global equities and airline margins. War-risk cover is being repriced.

The disruption has also removed about twenty percent of global L N G supply. That feeds directly into European gas and power prices.

Tehran is also threatening U.S. bases in Bulgaria and Cyprus. If those threats broaden, the conflict moves from energy shock to direct NATO contingency.

The trade is long energy, long tanker rates, long European defense, and short global equities on the tail risk of a NATO-Iran confrontation. Shipping owners are rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, adding days and freight cost.

To repeat: Brent is above ninety-one dollars because the world's most important oil chokepoint is effectively closed. Daily transits are down from about one hundred ten to a handful, and a handful is not enough.

The Round-Up

The dollar is under pressure from fiscal risk, not growth. Strategists at Saxo, Societe Generale, and Deutsche Bank say softer U.S. data and Fed ambiguity are eroding support. Saxo's Charu Chanana says a higher yield generated by a larger risk premium is not the same as one generated by strong fundamentals. Long-dollar positions are being cut.

In the UK, inflation hit a four-month high of two-point-nine percent in July as energy bills surged. The Bank of England held rates at three-point-seven-five percent on a six-to-three vote, with three members favoring hikes. Simon French of Panmure Liberum says policy is effectively rationing supply in land, energy, and capital.

Meta's trial opened in Oakland, with twenty-nine states seeking roughly two hundred billion dollars over child addiction to Instagram and Facebook. California's deputy attorney general said profits won when they conflicted with safety. The states want to end infinite scroll and like counts. Meta argues social media addiction does not exist.

Germany's gas storage is just forty-nine-point-nine percent full six weeks before heating season. Wholesale prices have doubled since February to sixty-three euros per megawatt hour. Reaching the November fill target would require one hundred forty-two L N G cargoes a month, but recent imports average one hundred five.

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing posted a record quarterly net profit of five-point-three-eight billion Hong Kong dollars. First-half profit rose twenty-four percent to a record ten-point-five-seven billion. State Grid's dim sum bond issue was oversubscribed thirteen times, and yuan usage through Hong Kong hit a six-month high.

A World Weather Attribution study finds human-caused climate change is the main driver of record European sea heatwaves, adding about two degrees Celsius to the Mediterranean. The average Mediterranean temperature in July was twenty-seven degrees Celsius, the highest on record. Marine heatwaves covered ninety percent of the Bay of Biscay.

OpenAI is pausing work on its Astra model over potentially critical cybersecurity risks. C E O Sam Altman says they would take action if capabilities outstripped safety. The Outlier

A startup called Nome is using AI to reduce a rare-disease diagnostic search from months to about ten minutes. It analyzes genetic results and identifies potential treatments.

Nome has reviewed roughly five thousand cases and found a viable treatment fit for twenty-five percent. It produces eighty to one hundred reports a month.

Each report runs fifty-three pages and lays out next steps. The platform currently oversees a little more than ten genetic medicine programs.

Jacalyn Lee of the D A N D Alliance says, 'We were handed this diagnosis, and little else. There was no roadmap, barely any research, no community that I was aware of at the time.'

Founder Stevie Ringel says they act with patient-level urgency, and a PhD is still in the loop before anything goes back to a patient.

About twenty-five percent of the work is automated now, and Nome expects to automate sixty to eighty percent within two years.

The dry observation: for families handed a diagnosis and little else, ten minutes may be the first roadmap they have had.
