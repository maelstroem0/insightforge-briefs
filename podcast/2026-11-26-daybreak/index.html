The holiday doesn't stop the compounding. I'm Iris, and this is The ApexQuantix Briefings. It's Thursday, November twenty-sixth. October P C E reaccelerated to its hottest reading in three years, and December rate-cut bets are gone — and South Korea's central bank just hiked into the AI boom. Lead Story

The US economy accelerated in the third quarter, and October inflation accelerated with it. The second estimate of third-quarter real G D P came in at four-point-three percent — above expectations. October headline P C E ran at three-point-eight percent year over year. Core P C E was three-point-three percent, the highest reading in three years.

Axios reported the economy is ripping. The Fed's Beige Book described continued growth alongside sticky prices. It flagged sustained cost pressure from energy, transportation, healthcare, insurance and tariffs.

For two months, the market had been pricing cuts into year-end. That positioning is now unwound, and unwinding is rarely orderly. December rate-cut bets are gone. What remains is a debate about how far the Fed goes, and how fast.

Higher expected policy rates raise the discount rate on long-duration equities and bonds. That lands hardest where the cash flows arrive late — unprofitable growth, long-dated technology, rate-sensitive small caps. The bond proxies feel it too — utilities, real estate, consumer staples. Equity multiples are where this shows up first, not earnings estimates.

It also lands on the Treasury curve, where the front end now carries a higher policy assumption. None of this is a shock to the AI capital cycle by itself. But it arrives in the same week that AI-linked equity and credit have both been repriced.

A cooler November print would change the arithmetic. So would a labour market that finally cracks. Neither is in evidence yet. For now, the burden of proof sits with the doves.

The forward marker is the Fed's eighth and ninth of December meeting. That's where the market learns whether this repricing is validated or walked back. The core fact, restated: the US economy is growing above trend, and inflation is not cooperating.

Second Story

Turning to Asia, where the tightening cycle has a new member. South Korea's central bank raised its key policy rate, resuming hikes after a pause. The Bank of Korea cited inflation. It also cited the AI boom lifting domestic growth — and it upgraded its 2027 growth outlook.

No hike size was disclosed, and no market-implied rate path was supplied. The move tightens Korean credit conditions. The channels to the won, to K O S P I equities and to Korean government bonds are real but conditional. They are not mechanical.

Korean assets sit unusually close to the global AI supply chain. Memory, advanced packaging, substrates, and the equipment that makes all three. That's why a growth upgrade and a rate hike arrive in the same breath. The Bank of Korea is tightening into a boom it can see, not one it is forecasting.

Seoul's benchmark index is a leveraged expression of the memory cycle. Rate hikes compress the multiple even when earnings hold up. That's the tension to watch. The growth upgrade says the demand is real. The hike says the price of that demand is inflation.

Both can be true at once, and that's the problem for a rate-setter. Watch two things from here. First, whether the central bank signals another move. Second, how the won carry trade responds.

A carry trade funded in a low-yielding currency loses its edge when that currency starts paying more. South Korea has been a funding leg for years. If that leg reprices, the unwind shows up first in the currency, then in cross-asset positioning. The read-through for global investors is straightforward.

The Round-Up

Here's the rest of the tape, and the rest of the world. Spot gold was down two-tenths of a percent at three thousand five hundred seventy-three dollars an ounce. It broke below three thousand five hundred dollars for the first time since September 2025. Markets are now pricing a sixty percent chance of a fifty-basis-point increase in January.

Germany's coalition adopted a 2027 federal budget with expenditure of almost five hundred sixty-one billion euros. Core net new borrowing is one hundred twenty-three billion euros, with total net new borrowing above two hundred billion euros through the decade.

The same budget cuts the electricity tax to the EU minimum for 2027, trimming power-price subsidies from sixteen-point-five to eleven-point-five-two billion euros. Heavy Bund issuance means supply pressure. Watch auction demand.

The F C C authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites for orbital AI data centers. Milestones require one hundred thousand satellites by November 2035 and the full constellation by 2040. The order overrode petitions to deny from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, and includes a deal for Nvidia's Vera Rubin processors.

A C B S News investigation found Nvidia sold advanced AI chips to at least two Chinese-owned US firms, including RiCloud AI and Aivres. Those firms exported at least five-point-six billion dollars of technology to Southeast Asia between April 2024 and February 2026. The US blacklisted Megaspeed on the twenty-fourth of November.

Yemen's Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery. Brent crude futures settled three-point-five percent higher at ninety-four dollars sixty-one a barrel.

Ofgem raised the UK energy price cap by twenty-six percent from the first of January 2027. That adds four hundred forty-three pounds a year, taking average annual household bills from one thousand seven hundred twenty-three pounds to two thousand one hundred sixty-six.

And a US-led consortium agreed to acquire the international assets of Russia's Lukoil. Dodgers owner Todd Boehly is reported to have clinched the deal. Watch regulatory approvals.

The Outlier

A governance oddity, and it carries a price. A review of financial disclosures shows Donald Trump's investment portfolios bought or sold Nvidia shares at least forty-four times this year. The portfolios held between twelve-and-a-half and fifty-eight million dollars of the stock at the end of 2025.

The transmission runs through the regulatory risk premium. Chip export policy and China enforcement both sit with this White House. The watchpoint is whether Congress opens an inquiry.
