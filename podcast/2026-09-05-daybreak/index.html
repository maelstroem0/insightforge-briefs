Lead Story

The world's fifty largest mining companies gained three hundred fifty-seven billion dollars in August — the biggest monthly increase on record. That push lifted their combined value back above two and a half trillion dollars for the first time since February. Gold itself is trading close to forty-five hundred an ounce, and it's holding there even after Friday's blowout payrolls report revived rate-hike bets.

A N Z puts the bid down to currency and central banks. Notably, in February — the last time the sector's value sat this high — gold traded about a thousand dollars an ounce above today's levels. The metal is now doing more work with less.

Why it matters. A record month in miners while yields climb is a market pricing currency debasement and policy confusion, not just inflation. That's a different driver than the rate trade, and it's why gold barely flinched when two-year yields jumped. Miners give you leverage to that thesis without buying the bond market's problem.

The watchpoint is Tuesday's C P I. A hot print validates the hike odds at sixty-five percent and could finally shake the metal. So — record month for the miners, three hundred fifty-seven billion dollars, and gold near forty-five hundred despite the strongest jobs report in months.

Second Story

Turning to Washington. President Trump threatened to halt trade with countries running surpluses against the United States unless the Federal Reserve cuts rates. That raises the Fed-independence premium on Treasuries and undercuts the dollar's haven bid. It also strengthens the case for gold hedges, which is exactly where this morning's lead is living.

Watch what the White House does, not what it posts. Credible follow-through would spike U.S. C D S and trade-war crosses overnight. Until then, this is pressure priced as noise.
