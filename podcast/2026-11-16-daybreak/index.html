Lead Story

Gold fell below thirty-nine hundred dollars an ounce, and silver dropped below forty-eight dollars to a one-year low, as markets priced a ninety-four percent chance of a December Fed rate hike.

That odds reading comes from C M E FedWatch. It followed October US consumer prices rising four tenths of a percent on the month. Rajeev Sharan, head of research at Brickwork Ratings, attributes the pullback to a stronger dollar and rising bond yields.

Global spot gold slipped from above four thousand one hundred dollars to around three thousand eight hundred eighty during the week. That's well below the third-quarter average of four thousand two hundred sixty-two dollars an ounce. Bullion fell four and a half percent last week.

Here's why this matters beyond the metals desk. Higher-for-longer US rate expectations raise the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays nothing. That transmission runs through real yields first, then the dollar, then every dollar-priced commodity.

Silver is the sharper signal. It sits at a one-year low and carries industrial demand on top of the monetary bid. Research cited in the reporting expects silver demand to fall five percent in 2026, as weaker jewellery demand and substitution in solar cells bite. High prices have accelerated that substitution.

A stronger greenback makes dollar-denominated bullion more expensive for buyers holding other currencies. And higher real yields tighten conditions for emerging-market currencies generally. Indian wedding demand and central-bank buying are cited in the reporting as downside limiters.

What to watch: remarks from Fed Chair Warsh and the minutes of the last meeting. Both land before the December decision, and both can move the real-yield leg of this trade. The core fact to carry: gold below thirty-nine hundred, silver below forty-eight, and a market that now puts ninety-four percent odds on a December hike.

Second Story

Turning to Nvidia, where the board is under pressure to reinstate the chief executive it lost. Jensen Huang resigned after an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, according to Axios. The shares have fallen near thirty percent over the last month, on that same reporting.

Reports say Huang is now discussing a return. No board statement and no timeline for a decision has been supplied. The number that turns this from a governance story into a market story is eight percent. That's Nvidia's rough weight in the S and P 500.

Tom Graff of Facet puts the figure at about fourteen percent of growth-style index funds, and twenty-five to thirty percent of some tech and semiconductor funds. He also estimates that about forty percent of the S and P 500 is tied to the AI buildout.

Here's the consequence. A leadership vacuum at the largest index constituent isn't a single-stock problem — it's a passive-portfolio problem. Every dollar tracking the S and P 500 carries that weight, whether the holder chose it or not. In growth and semiconductor funds, the concentration is heavier still.

On the reported detail: Singapore-based Megaspeed said Alice Huang left the firm last year. One report put Nvidia's executive insurance cover at nine hundred million dollars and flagged a governance gap. Those are reported details, not statements from the company.

Leadership uncertainty at a bellwether chip supplier conditions sentiment across the whole AI data-centre complex, from chipmakers to the power and cooling suppliers behind them. The next test is Tuesday's quarterly results, and specifically the AI capital-spending guidance. That's where the market gets a number instead of a headline. For now, the board hasn't decided, and the index weight hasn't changed.

The Round-Up

China confirmed Monday that Xi Jinping will meet Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump in Shenzhen on November nineteenth. It's the first three-way gathering of the leaders of the world's three foremost nuclear powers, on the sidelines of A P E C. The group has advanced to about sixty kilometres from Aden. Riyadh has rerouted roughly four million barrels a day of crude through the East-West pipeline to Yanbu — about four percent of global supply.

India's October retail inflation accelerated to six-point-oh-nine percent from five-point-five-four, breaching the R B I's six percent tolerance band for the first time in two years. The Sensex fell about four tenths of a percent on Friday, and the Nifty shed six tenths. The R B I meets on December fourth. Foreign investors sold about sixty-one billion dollars of Indian equities from September 2024 through October, and have bought back roughly eight billion since mid-October.

Anthropic is set to debut on the Nasdaq at a target valuation of up to one-point-four trillion dollars, with proceeds potentially exceeding ninety billion. Pricing is expected November eighteenth.

Diamondback is nearing a five billion dollar acquisition to expand drilling in the Permian Basin, according to the Financial Times. The target, the structure and the financing are not stated. It's the latest in a wave of shale consolidation that has accelerated amid volatile energy prices.

And Asia holds sixty-six of the ninety-one gigawatts of nuclear capacity under construction globally — seventy-three percent, with China alone above forty-three gigawatts. Spot uranium is near a nine-month high around ninety-two dollars fifty a pound.

The Outlier

One more, and it's the cleanest tell on risk appetite. Bitcoin fell to about sixty-two thousand dollars over the weekend, its weakest since July, as US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly two-point-three-five billion dollars of net outflows over the two weeks ending November thirteenth. Liquidations totalled two-point-oh-two billion. Ether and Solana each lost more than nineteen percent.

The transmission is straightforward. Crypto is the highest-beta expression of the same hawkish repricing that's hitting gold. Watch whether the weekend low holds, and whether E T F redemptions slow.
