Lead Story

The dollar is heading into December on its third straight monthly gain.

The Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index is up one-point-nine percent in November. That puts it on track for a third consecutive monthly advance. That's the haven bid and the rate bid arriving together.

Traders now fully price a quarter-point Federal Reserve hike next month. The dollar index rose a quarter of a percent to just above one hundred four on Friday, and it was headed for a weekly gain of about two-tenths of a percent.

As the reporting frames it, two forces are doing the work. A rupture in the AI trade has pushed capital toward the safest currency. Rising rate expectations have done the rest. The dollar is supported against G10 peers by both, and the monthly streak now runs to three.

That combination cuts the other way for US equities. A stronger dollar and a higher discount rate both press on valuations, and the pressure lands hardest on AI-linked technology. The same rate path that lifts the currency is the one that re-prices long-duration growth.

The message for an investor is narrow. The market has stopped arguing about whether the Fed hikes. It's arguing about what comes after.

The forward calendar is short and dense. November payrolls come first, and they matter more than usual with the December decision fully priced. The December Fed meeting settles the rest. Any softness in the labour data would be the first real test of that assumption, and the first thing that would take the dollar lower.

Restating the core fact. The dollar is up one-point-nine percent this month, on track for a third consecutive monthly gain, with a quarter-point hike fully priced for December.

Second Story

Turning to Europe. S and P Global Ratings cut its outlook on France to negative on Thursday. It acted hours after I N S E E confirmed the country has slipped into a technical recession.

The statistics agency reported a contraction of two-tenths of a percent in third-quarter output. S and P kept France at A-plus for the long term and A-one short term. The outlook is where the warning sits.

S and P cited three things: rising public debt, moderate growth, and political fragmentation that extends beyond the 2027 presidential election. Those are the agency's words, and they describe a fiscal problem with a political ceiling on it.

There's a second data point in the same release cycle. French inflation slowed to two-point-nine percent year on year in November, I N S E E reported. Slower prices ease the household squeeze. They don't ease the borrowing arithmetic.

October brought the cost-of-living riots and the dry fuel pumps. November brought the recession call and the outlook cut.

The consequence sits in sovereign funding. French O A T spreads face upward pressure, with a conditional read-through to French banks and to euro-area sovereign credit generally. It runs through one channel: the price of holding French duration.

The path at stake is the deficit. The Banque de France has mapped a return to three percent of G D P by 2029, from five-point-one percent in 2025. A negative outlook makes that map harder to sell to the people who buy the paper.

The next markers are concrete. Watch the 2027 budget. Watch whether the five percent deficit path holds. Both land before the presidential cycle does.

The core fact again. S and P moved France's outlook to negative the same day I N S E E confirmed a technical recession, and the O A T spread is where that gets priced.
