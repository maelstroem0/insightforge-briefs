Billboard

Sunday morning, and the news came in by water before the futures could price it. I'm Iris, and this is The ApexQuantix Briefings. It's Sunday, November fifteenth. Three tankers burned on mines in the Strait of Hormuz — and Fed officials are openly floating a bigger December hike. Plus, stocks up, yields up, bitcoin down hard.

Lead Story

Iran's navy says three oil tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday after striking naval mines. That's three on Saturday alone — after two more tankers hit mines in the same waterway on Friday. Five vessels in two days, at the world's most important oil chokepoint.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps Navy said on November thirteenth that a liquefied gas carrier transiting under a US fighter-jet escort, along with two oil tankers, struck mines and caught fire while using what it called unauthorized routes. Iran's regular Artesh Navy separately claimed on Saturday that another tanker hit a mine east of the strait.

Set that against the record. US Central Command announced on August twenty-seventh that American forces had completely demined the strait. The Iranian claims have not been independently verified, and they arrive with no price, no flow data, and no verified damage assessment attached.

The transmission channel here is transit, not production. Iran is restricting vessel passage to pressure Washington, and each strike raises the cost of moving crude through Hormuz — tanker freight, war-risk insurance, and an owner's willingness to sail at all. Those costs land in delivered barrel economics before they land in the flat price.

There's a second-order channel too. Gulf producers can reroute, but the alternatives add days and fuel burn, and the insurance market prices a chokepoint on the quality of the last month, not the last decade. A week of steady mine strikes does more to the war-risk curve than a single explosion ever does.

Watch two things this week. Whether underwriters reprice Hormuz transit cover, and whether any confirmed disruption to actual loadings shows up in the flow data rather than in the headlines. The core fact is simple — tankers are burning in the strait, five in two days, on Iranian accounts that remain unverified.

Second Story

Turning to the Fed. Policymakers have floated the idea of a larger rate increase in December, citing strong jobs, growth and inflation data. The Financial Times report doesn't say how much larger, and it doesn't say where the federal funds rate currently sits.

That is single-source coverage, and independent corroboration hasn't been established. Treat the size as unconfirmed. The direction, though, is consistent with a debate that's been running since October, when two Fed deputies intervened to pull the odds of an October hike down to roughly a quarter from seventy percent.

The channel is the front end of the curve. A bigger December increase would steepen a tightening path the market has already begun to price, and it would ratchet the discount rate underneath every long-duration equity multiple. Rate-sensitive sectors — utilities, REITs, unprofitable growth — carry the first mark.

The dollar is the second channel. A faster US tightening path widens rate differentials against Europe and Japan, and that pressure shows up in import-cost math for emerging markets long before it shows up in an FOMC statement. The report supplies no market print, so don't read Friday's tape as a verdict on this story.

What to watch is the December statement itself, and the projections that come with it. The size is the headline, but the dot plot is the story. And note what hasn't changed — nothing is decided, no size is on the record, and this is a floated idea rather than a signaled commitment.

Markets Check

Let's get to the markets, as of the November fifteenth print. The S and P 500 was up two and a half percent on the week. The Nasdaq 100 added three and a half. The Russell 2000 gained one-point-three. The vix fell almost twenty-five percent.

Treasuries sold off. The two-year yield rose forty basis points, the ten-year twenty-nine, the thirty-year twenty-two. The dollar was up one-point-one percent on the dollar index. Brent fell two and a half percent. Gold dropped four-point-six. And bitcoin fell almost fourteen percent.

The Round-Up

Egypt's final review under its eight-billion-dollar I M F program is moving online with about two-point-three billion dollars still at stake. The mission to Cairo ran from October twenty-fifth to November fifth and broke up without a staff-level agreement. The program expires December fifteenth, and the Fund has given no timeline for follow-up talks.

US intelligence figures show Iran still holds up to two thousand five hundred missiles, contradicting President Trump's public assertions. Sources say Trump has told senior advisers he may hold off on attacking Iran absent new provocations from Tehran or the Houthis. A third US aircraft carrier is moving into the region.

The F A A's nationwide ground stop on Friday came after an L3Harris router upgrade overwhelmed air traffic control communications. Seventeen of twenty-two air route traffic control centers saw instability on voice and ground links. FlightAware counted more than four thousand six hundred cancellations and thirteen thousand delays as of late Saturday morning. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy raised his modernization funding request to fifty billion dollars.

Zelensky says US negotiators raised, for the first time, whether Ukraine would withdraw from the Donbas in exchange for NATO membership. Kyiv says it won't cede territory, and would instead put a package of security guarantees, a funded army and reconstruction money to a national referendum. The US hasn't confirmed the proposal. Overnight, Russia launched one hundred twenty-three drones at Ukraine, seventy-three of them jet-powered.

Shokeir Wind Energy reached financial close on a nine-hundred-megawatt wind farm near Ras Shokeir in the Gulf of Suez, with lenders committing about five hundred eighty-eight million dollars of non-recourse debt. Engie owns thirty-five percent, Aeolus forty, and Orascom Construction twenty-five. It will be Engie's largest onshore wind farm anywhere once running.

And Germany has shortlisted a Brookfield-C P P, Equinor and Centrica consortium to bid for a seventy-five percent stake in gas supplier Uniper. Berlin would keep twenty-five percent and a veto right. An R W E-K K R grouping and E P Group dropped out. Bid values haven't been disclosed.

The Outlier

US diesel fell below six dollars a gallon, a two-month low. A gallon cost six dollars nineteen cents on October fifth and six twenty-nine on November second. Before the Middle East crisis, it was around three seventy-five. The Trump-Putin accord promises one-point-eight million tons of Russian diesel, but as of November thirteenth none had been registered entering the US — and one tanker reversed course four hundred nautical miles from New York. Watch the first verified cargo berth.
