American stocks closed their worst week in more than eighteen months, with the Nasdaq down ten percent — and Taiwan's regulator banned short sales below the prior close after a thirty-five-year weekly slump. Plus, markets in three words: sold, broad, everywhere.

Lead Story

US equities closed Friday with their worst week in more than eighteen months. And the selling was led by the company that had just reported the best numbers in its history.

The S and P 500 fell three-point-seven percent Friday and eight-point-six percent for the week. The Nasdaq lost five-point-three percent Friday and ten percent on the week.

Nvidia fell eleven-point-six percent Friday, despite quarterly revenue of a hundred fifteen-point-eight billion dollars. That revenue is up a hundred three percent from a year earlier, and guidance for the current quarter is about a hundred thirty billion.

Extraordinary numbers, and the stock was sold. That divergence is the story, and it has stopped being a chip story.

The selling spread into industrials, autos, luxury and banks. In Paris, the C A C 40 lost five-point-three percent on the week, with the same sectors among the decliners.

That breadth is what separates a de-rating from a rotation out of one crowded trade. And the transmission channel now runs through financing.

Tighter financial conditions raise the cost of capital for the borrowers funding AI infrastructure with debt. Scrutiny has moved with them.

It began on chips and demand. It now sits on financing and disclosure — a slower test, and a harder one to pass before the numbers arrive.

Nothing in the reported results broke. What broke was the willingness to pay for those results in advance.

The next test is Wednesday, when the P C E inflation print lands. The question into that print is whether hyperscaler capital-spending guidance holds at current levels.

So carry one fact into Monday's open. The index leader fell on record results, the Nasdaq lost ten percent on the week, and the correction is no longer about a single sector.

Second Story

Turning to Taipei. Taiwan's benchmark suffered its worst weekly fall in thirty-five years, and the regulator answered over the weekend with emergency measures.

The Financial Supervisory Commission banned short-selling below the prior close. It also raised the minimum margin requirement for short sales to a hundred fifty percent from a hundred thirty.

Alongside that, it activated a stabilization fund of five hundred billion New Taiwan dollars — about fifteen billion in US currency. Taiwanese stocks slumped six-point-two percent on Friday alone.

China's side of the same trade is larger. Its AI stocks have lost one-point-seven trillion dollars in this selloff, and the S T A R 50 index is down forty-four percent from its summer high.

For scale, the Nasdaq is eighteen percent below its record. Beijing's exposure is different from Taipei's, and it runs through policy.

Equities are the intended replacement for the property channel that used to hold household savings. A forty-four percent drawdown makes that replacement harder to sell.

October data showed property investment sank nearly twenty-two percent. That is the gap the equity market was meant to fill.

For investors, the read-through runs to semiconductor supply chains and Asia technology indices, where the same de-rating has already applied.

There is a counterweight, and it's a real one. October export orders rose seventy-eight percent from a year earlier, data Friday showed.

Order books at that pace against a thirty-five-year weekly slump is the divergence to watch. Monday's session tells you whether the curbs hold the tape.

The Round-Up

In Brussels, the EU and Indonesia signed a trade and investment agreement after a decade of talks. It removes tariffs on ninety-eight-point-five percent of goods categories and exempts about ninety percent of Indonesian exports from EU duties.

Brussels projects EU exports to Indonesia up nearly forty percent within fifteen years, with annual duty savings above six hundred million euros. Ratification is pending, with provisional application targeted for early 2027.

European gas is holding a weather premium. Dutch T T F December futures stood near eighty-seven euros twenty-eight a megawatt hour on Friday afternoon, after reaching a hundred three-point-nine euros in late October.

Storage went into winter at its lowest in more than a decade, and an unusually early breakdown of the stratospheric polar vortex is the risk traders are carrying.

Bitcoin slid below fifty-seven thousand dollars to a two-year low near fifty-five thousand one hundred. About two-point-four billion dollars of crypto positions were liquidated in twenty-four hours, catching more than three hundred forty-three thousand traders.

No tonnage or price figures were disclosed, so the scale of that physical re-routing stays unquantified.
