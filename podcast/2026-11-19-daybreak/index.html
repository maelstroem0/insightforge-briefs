Rates did the talking overnight, and everything else took notes. I'm Iris, and this is The ApexQuantix Briefings. It's Thursday, November nineteenth.

A Fed governor wants a half-point December hike and the dollar has hit a nineteen-month high — and Iran widens its fire to Kuwait after US strikes on Qeshm Island. Plus, stocks heavy, gold smashed, crypto lower.

Lead Story

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller wants a half-percentage-point rate increase at the December meeting. The dollar index rose three-tenths of a percent to 103.87, its highest since April of last year.

Waller says inflation is still headed in the wrong direction. He says the central bank should consider a larger, half-point move when it meets on December eighth and ninth.

The October FOMC minutes reinforced the message. Officials saw growing inflation risk, and most expected further rate moves ahead. Futures fully price a quarter-point increase in December.

The odds of the bigger half-point move have risen to seven-point-three percent, from five-point-three percent before the minutes landed. That's the number that matters. The tail got fatter.

Gold fell three-point-two percent to three thousand, seven hundred twenty-six dollars an ounce, a third straight day of declines. Silver dropped five percent to forty-five dollars and three cents, after touching its lowest since September of last year.

Platinum fell four-point-one percent to one thousand, three hundred seventy dollars and seventy cents. Palladium slid two-point-two percent to nine hundred forty-nine dollars.

The euro was down three-tenths of a percent, just under one dollar ten, briefly touching its lowest since April of last year. The yen was about flat, near one hundred fifty-nine to the dollar, after Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda reiterated the need to tighten policy further.

Bitcoin lost two-point-five percent to sixty-two thousand, six hundred eighty-eight dollars, inching toward the four-month low it touched on Sunday.

For an equities-led investor, the transmission runs through real yields. A firmer front end pressures long-duration growth names first. Non-yielding assets answer immediately, and that is what gold, silver and crypto are doing.

The core fact again: a sitting Fed governor is publicly arguing for a half-point hike in December, and the market is starting to price it. Watch the December eighth and ninth meeting for whether the committee follows.

Second Story

Turning to the Gulf. Kuwait says it repelled its first Iranian attack in more than two months. The strike came after US fighter jets hit a Revolutionary Guard missile launch site on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz.

Bahrain reported a third strike in five days. An Adnoc tanker was hit, leaving one crew member missing and eleven injured.

During the war, Ali Al Salem Air Base was struck at least thirty times by Iran. That damaged or destroyed at least fifteen aircraft hangars, along with warehouses and ten buildings near a residential area, according to an Associated Press report.

The previous day, coalition forces launched a major air attack across rebel-held territory in Yemen, which the Houthis said killed at least seventeen people.

For portfolios, the channel that matters is the Strait itself. It is the chokepoint for Gulf crude and for Qatari liquefied natural gas.

Every escalation that pulls another Gulf state into the exchange raises the insurance and freight cost of moving barrels through it. Tanker rates and war-risk premiums show that first.

The crude benchmark shows it second, and the risk premium there has been rebuilt and unwound repeatedly this year. The distinction worth holding: the attacks are real, and the price response is not automatic.

Iran is now striking US-linked targets in two Gulf states in the same week. That is a wider set of participants than the market was pricing a quarter ago.

Watch the next round of strikes, and whether commercial shipping in the Strait starts pricing a wider war. The core fact again: Kuwait repelled an Iranian attack for the first time in more than two months, and Bahrain was hit for the third time in five days.

The Round-Up

Start with the listing of the year. Anthropic priced its initial public offering at eighty-three dollars a share, above its seventy-to-seventy-eight dollar range.

The Claude maker sold about nine hundred eighty million shares to raise roughly eighty-one-point-three billion dollars, valuing it near one-point-five trillion. That is the largest US listing on record, topping SpaceX's seventy-five billion dollar IPO in June.

Shares begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker A N T. The company reported run-rate revenue of roughly eighty-one billion dollars at the end of October, and a five hundred sixty-seven million dollar operating loss in the third quarter.

To Australia, where the labour market cracked. Unemployment jumped to four-point-nine percent in October from four-point-seven, the highest in five years.

Employment fell forty-three thousand five hundred, and full-time jobs dropped two thousand nine hundred. Youth unemployment rose to eleven-point-three percent.

National Australia Bank says markets have priced out a sixth R B A rate increase and now factor in a cut by September of next year.

In China, Tencent's third-quarter profit flatlined as capital expenditure jumped more than sixfold year-on-year on AI investment. Revenue rose eight percent and cloud services thirty-five.

President Martin Lau said the new US G P U ban sets everyone back in China. Tencent's agentic assistant, WorkBuddy, has ten million users consuming over one hundred trillion tokens a day.

In Michigan, Ford started battery cell production at its three billion dollar Marshall plant last month, using C A T L-licensed L F P technology.

The plant is expected to produce forty cells a minute, or twenty gigawatt-hours a year, down from initial plans of up to forty gigawatt-hours.

On fiscal policy, the Bipartisan Policy Center forecasts Washington could hit its forty-one-point-one trillion dollar debt ceiling as early as February or March of 2027.

Treasury cash and extraordinary measures could delay default until August through December of that year. A divided Congress raises the risk of another showdown over the borrowing limit.

And in critical minerals, the US-backed Lobito Corridor has attracted more than seven hundred fifty million dollars to move D R Congo's copper and cobalt to Angola's Atlantic coast.

The Port of Lobito handled about one-point-eight million tonnes of cargo in 2025, of which around one hundred fifty-eight thousand tonnes was minerals. The corridor's railway carried a record one-point-six million passengers.

The Outlier

Tuttle Capital and Defiance ETFs have filed for the first exchange-traded funds targeting credit default swaps on AI-linked borrowers, from CoreWeave and Oracle to the Magnificent Seven.

CoreWeave's nine-point-seven-five percent bond due 2031 yields close to sixteen percent, and its five-year C D S recently traded between eleven hundred and twelve hundred. Oracle's C D S has moved from below eighty to above three hundred in a year.

If approved, that gives equity investors a hedging channel against the AI capital-spending cycle. Watch those spreads.
