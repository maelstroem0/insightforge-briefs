LEAD STORY
Reuters reports that Asian equities are heading for their worst week since 2020, as the global selloff in AI-linked shares deepens across the region. That's the sharpest weekly decline for Asian stocks since 2020, and it spans regional markets rather than one index.
The reporting carries no magnitude for the move, and no sector breakdown. What it does show is breadth: Indian stocks slipped from nine-month highs at the open, and the weakness ran across the region rather than sitting in a single market.
There's an index-construction point in that. When the largest weights are the ones being sold, a benchmark falls faster than its average member, so the index move can look broader than the selling actually is.
For an equities-led global investor, the consequence is where the damage lands. Asian benchmarks carry heavy weight in semiconductors, hardware and platform names, so a de-rating in AI exposure transmits to index performance directly, not at the margin.
That's the channel that turns a US technology story into a regional equity story. If the AI-trade gloom persists, Asian equity indices and AI-linked sectors face continued pressure — that's the conditional read, not a forecast.
No consensus or valuation data has been supplied to size the move, so the honest read is direction, not magnitude. A week like this also does something to positioning: it forces allocators to ask whether regional exposure is delivering diversification, or a cheaper route to the same AI beta.
Either way, the driver named in the reporting is the AI rout itself, not a local catalyst. What to watch is the weekly close, and whether the next session in Asia stabilizes or extends the decline.
The core fact, once more: Asian shares are headed for their worst week since 2020, and the AI rout is now a regional story.

SECOND STORY
Turning to the AI trade's first real-time test in the public market. Anthropic raised eighty-one billion dollars in its initial public offering, the largest listing on record, surpassing SpaceX's seventy-five-billion-dollar deal.
The shares closed seventeen percent above the offer price on day one, briefly valuing the Claude maker at two-point-zero-one trillion dollars before finishing near one-point-seven-five trillion. In extended trading, they gave back three percent.
That sequence is the story. Demand for the deal was real, and the aftermarket's conviction was thinner. Anthropic is the first leading AI model developer to list, so its trading is the cleanest available read on public appetite for AI equity risk.
A stable aftermarket would ease pressure on AI-linked equities and their suppliers. Continued weakness reinforces the de-rating already underway. The business behind the listing isn't the problem.
Anthropic reported more than eleven thousand nine hundred customers spending at least a hundred thousand dollars a year on a trailing twelve-month basis as of the end of September. That's up from about fifteen hundred in 2025.
Chief executive Dario Amodei's two hundred thirty-seven-point-three million shares at eighty-three dollars apiece now carry a valuation near nineteen-point-seven billion dollars. The debut also briefly put Anthropic among the most valuable public companies in the world.
The next test is whether the shares hold above the offer price in the coming sessions, and whether the rest of the AI complex takes its cue from that.
The core fact: the largest IPO ever priced into a tape that was already selling AI, and the first session closed well above the offer but below the intraday peak.
