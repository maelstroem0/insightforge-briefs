LEAD STORY
Houthi missile and drone strikes shut Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport over the weekend.
At least sixteen people were killed, including a U.S. citizen, and roughly three hundred were wounded. The group also hit oil facilities and Dammam airport in eastern Saudi Arabia.
Saudi Arabia's energy minister said Monday that the country had been tested and would mount a quote, serious, unquote response.
More than two thousand flights have been cancelled to and from King Khalid since Thursday, according to FlightAware. Cirium data showed around sixty per cent of scheduled flights there were expected to be cancelled Monday.
Brent crude surged to near two-week highs above one hundred and six dollars a barrel. Saudi Arabia has launched a probe into how its air defences failed to intercept the barrage.
That keeps a Middle East risk premium firmly inside crude and refined products. Aviation-disruption costs are already real for carriers routed through the Gulf.
Higher energy prices feed straight back into headline inflation, which is the path the Federal Reserve keeps telling you it is watching. Rate-sensitive equity multiples sit on the other side of that trade.
The strikes hit infrastructure, and no Aramco production impact has been reported. That distinction matters, because it separates a premium from a shortage.
Watch two things from here. Whether Saudi air defences can be rebuilt quickly, and whether Washington intervenes directly.
Riyadh's airport remains shut, and Brent is still above a hundred and six dollars. That is the number every importer is now pricing against.

SECOND STORY
Turning to HCLTech, which reported second-quarter results and raised its full-year outlook. The Indian IT services bellwether posted net profit of five thousand one hundred thirty-two crore rupees, up twenty-one point two per cent from a year earlier.
Revenue came in at three point seven two billion dollars, up two per cent sequentially. That is the first sequential growth after two quarters of decline.
Management raised FY27 constant-currency revenue growth guidance to two point five to four per cent, from one to four per cent. It also declared an interim dividend of twelve rupees a share.
New orders were three point three billion dollars in the quarter. First-half bookings reached five point six seven billion, which management called a record for the period.
AI-related revenue reached an annualized eight hundred seventy-six million dollars.
Here is the channel that makes this a market story, not just a print. Indian IT services is the cleanest read on whether enterprise AI budgets are converting into contracted work.
A guidance raise after two declining quarters says the conversion is happening at the margin. It says nothing yet about pricing, and management has flagged AI-led deflation as the thing to watch.
The tape was less convinced than the release. HCLTech shares rose about half a per cent to one thousand two hundred twenty-three rupees on the N S E, while the Nifty IT index ended the day one point one per cent lower.
Analyst targets span one thousand eighty to one thousand three hundred thirty rupees. That spread is the honest picture of an uncertain demand cycle.
Watch whether peers confirm the bookings trend, and whether the AI-deflation commentary intensifies. The record date for that dividend is October sixteenth.

THE ROUND-UP
In China, the peak car-buying season hit the brakes. Passenger-vehicle retail sales fell twenty-eight per cent in September to one point six two million units, a twelfth straight monthly decline, per the China Passenger Car Association.
Exports surged almost seventy-four per cent to nine hundred seventeen thousand units. That is thirty-six per cent of manufacturers' sales, up from nineteen per cent a year earlier. Volkswagen group deliveries fell fourteen per cent.
India's September consumer price inflation accelerated to five point five four per cent from four point eight two. That is above the five point four consensus and a twenty-two-month high. Food inflation climbed to seven point seven four per cent from five point nine seven.
The Reserve Bank of India tightened further. It limited overseas clients on rupee non-deliverable contracts to hedging only, and widened a twenty per cent cash reserve requirement to banks' own rupee hedges.
The ten-year benchmark yield touched seven point four per cent intraday, its highest since March 2023. The rupee logged its steepest single-day gain in more than six months.
Washington's Russian diesel waiver is now in force, covering up to three million tons. Three hundred thousand tons for October, five hundred thousand for November, a million thereafter.
Treasury's sanctions relief runs to April seventh, 2027, and diesel averages about six dollars twenty-five a gallon. A bipartisan discharge petition in the House is trying to block the purchases.
The UK heads into an October twenty-eighth budget with credibility on the line. The I M F lifted its 2026 UK growth forecast by three tenths to one point three per cent, crediting larger AI investment.
But the Bank of England still expects UK inflation slightly above four per cent in the first quarter of 2027. Ebury warns UK assets could underperform into the budget.
European tech funding rebounded hard in September. Fifteen point eight billion euros across three hundred deals, up from three point two billion and a hundred sixty-five deals in August.
The UK took seven point three billion of that, cloud was the top-funded sector at five point five billion, and exit activity held near thirty-nine transactions.

THE OUTLIER
One warning worth a moment. The I M F said Tuesday that sovereign bond yields could rise in a disorderly fashion.
Hyperscalers now account for ten per cent of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from less than one per cent since 2024. Hedge funds hold about nine per cent of U.S. Treasuries, versus four per cent in 2022.
That is the AI credit channel meeting sovereign supply. Watch maturity extension and rollover risk, where collateral values get tested first.
