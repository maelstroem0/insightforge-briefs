SECOND STORY
Turning to Nvidia, which has told manufacturing partners to pause server shipments into Southeast Asia. The instruction covers Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, and applies to partners including Dell and Super Micro.
The pause follows a new US rule requiring licences for advanced AI chip purchases in those four countries. That rule also closed a remote-access loophole that Chinese firms had been using to reach restricted compute.
Nvidia hasn't said how long the halt lasts. The reporting — from the Wall Street Journal, the New York Times and TechCrunch — doesn't quantify the financial impact.
That's the first thing to note: this is an unspecified pause, not a cancellation.
Why it matters: Southeast Asia has been one of the fastest-growing destinations for AI data-center construction. A licensing regime that reaches the server, not just the chip, changes the compliance burden for every integrator shipping into the region.
The rule's target is remote access — the workaround where restricted buyers rent compute they can't buy. Closing it shifts enforcement from where the chip lands to where the rack lands.
Dell and Super Micro carry the shipping exposure directly. Nvidia's runs through its server supply chain, into four of the region's largest AI infrastructure markets.
The read-through is conditional. Duration is unknown, and so is the dollar figure. If the pause lifts within weeks, it's a compliance reset. If it extends, it becomes a demand question for the regional buildout.
Washington's export-control perimeter has widened from the chip to the rack, and that widening is the actual news here.
Watch whether the pause is lifted or extended — and whether other jurisdictions get folded into the licence requirement.
