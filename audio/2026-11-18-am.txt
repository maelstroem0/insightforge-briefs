LEAD STORY
The US ten-year Treasury yield has held above five percent for a twenty-seventh day this year — and investors are positioning for the Federal Reserve to tighten again.
Fed funds futures traders were pricing a roughly ninety-eight percent chance of a twenty-five-basis-point hike at the December eighth and ninth meeting, according to the C M E Group's FedWatch Tool. The same market put only about a two percent chance on fifty basis points.
The ten-year traded near five-point-oh-nine percent on Tuesday. The trigger was October inflation, which came in hotter than expected and pushed markets to factor in additional rate hikes.
The consequence runs through everything priced off the long end. Thirty-year mortgage rates have stayed above seven percent since mid-September, so housing and consumer credit stay tight. Higher discount rates also press on equity valuations, especially for long-duration growth names. Credit spreads, homebuilder equities and utility valuations all key off that front end.
Even so, the long end has drawn a haven bid as equities have fallen. US stocks dropped more than one percent on Tuesday. A firming front end and a supported long end — that's the repricing now moving through global duration.
Positioning has shifted accordingly. The debate is no longer whether the Fed is finished; it's how far it goes, and how long the ten-year stays above five. For an equities-led investor the arithmetic is simple: a higher risk-free rate lowers the present value of future earnings.
Hold onto one number: twenty-seven. That's how many days this year the ten-year has spent above five percent.
The next test is the December eighth and ninth meeting. Futures currently treat a quarter-point hike there as close to a done deal.

SECOND STORY
Turning to the biggest public-market test of the AI trade this year. Anthropic is guiding prospective investors that it expects to price its shares at the top of its seventy-to-seventy-eight dollar range, or above.
Demand is the reason. Orders are reported at nearly seven times the shares available. Filings cover eight hundred twenty-five million shares and a potential ninety-point-four-billion-dollar raise, including a twenty-six-billion-dollar private placement.
Amazon is set to hold fourteen-point-seven percent. Google thirteen-point-seven percent. And Anthropic has committed to spend more than one hundred billion dollars on cloud services over the next decade.
That last figure is the read-through. A debut priced at the top of the range resets the comparable for every private AI lab, and it validates the cloud commitments that sit behind AI infrastructure spending.
It also tests the equity risk premium on AI itself. If the offering clears at seven times coverage and trades up, the market is saying it will fund AI capital spending at these valuations. If it breaks issue, that signal reverses quickly.
The stakes are larger than one company. Anthropic's own economics are tied to cloud capacity it doesn't own, and its two largest shareholders are also among its largest suppliers.
This is the same book that was six times covered at the start of the week. Demand has tightened, not loosened.
Watch the pricing, then the first session of trading. Whether the shares hold above the offer price will set the tone for the next wave of AI listings.
