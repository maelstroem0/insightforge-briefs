LEAD STORY
October consumer prices rose four tenths of a percent on a seasonally adjusted basis. That's the third consecutive outsized increase, and it came in above what economists had forecast.
Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the print borderline disturbing. Fed Governor Christopher Waller says a half-point move in December should be on the table if core inflation ran near three tenths. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan sees two more quarter-point increases.
The pricing followed. Fed funds futures now assign roughly a fifteen percent probability to a half-point hike in December. They put about sixty percent odds on the target range reaching four-and-a-quarter to four-and-a-half percent by January.
Why it matters is the discount rate. A hotter core print lifts the front end of the curve, and the front end discounts every long-duration cash flow in the market.
Rate-sensitive equities take the hit first — anything whose valuation leans on terminal growth. The two-year yield sets the tone, the dollar follows, and gold's bid depends on whether real yields rise faster than inflation expectations.
The read-through isn't uniform. Three straight outsized monthly increases suggest the disinflation trend has stalled rather than reversed. That distinction decides whether the Fed stops at a quarter point or goes fifty in December.
Watch the December FOMC, and watch the next C P I print before it. If core comes in at three tenths again, the half-point case stops being a tail risk and becomes the debate.
The core fact bears repeating. October C P I rose four tenths of a percent, the third straight outsized increase, and a half-point December hike is now a live probability rather than a hypothetical.

SECOND STORY
Turning to Nvidia. The board is reportedly preparing to remove chief executive Jensen Huang. The Wall Street Journal reported the move after a second allegation of an affair with a subordinate, following an existing board investigation into Huang's ties to an alleged Chinese spy.
The market's answer came after hours. Nvidia fell more than eight percent to a hundred fifty-eight dollars and seventy-eight cents. Micron dropped nearly two percent, CoreWeave six percent, and Nasdaq 100 futures fell about one percent.
The wider channel is leadership risk at the bellwether. Nvidia had already lost seven percent this week before that after-hours move, and it's on pace for a sixth straight weekly decline.
Roughly six hundred billion dollars of market value has come off since the board opened its investigation. That's the equity that anchors AI beta — suppliers, memory, the whole compute chain reads its direction.
What's not established is succession. No named interim, no timeline, no statement of what a new chief executive would do with the capital plan. Until that's clear, the read-through to suppliers is sentiment, not orders.
The next observable test is the board's confirmation, then Nvidia's earnings on November seventeenth. Those two dates decide whether this is a governance event or a strategy event.
For an index investor the arithmetic is simple. Nvidia's weight means a leadership vacuum at the top is a beta problem before it's a company problem. Suppliers get marked on the same headline, whether or not their order books change.
So: a board preparing to remove its chief executive, a stock down more than eight percent after hours, and a sixth straight weekly slide. The confirmation is what matters now.
