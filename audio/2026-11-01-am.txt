SECOND STORY
Turning to Tehran, where the war-risk premium is being written rather than paid.
Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has ordered armed forces to prepare an immediate, geographically unrestricted response to any new US or Israeli attack.
That order came at his first disclosed meeting with top commanders since the war began.
Qatari mediators held more than twelve hours of talks in Tehran on Friday, working to restart direct US-Iran negotiations and avert a resumption of American-led strikes after the US midterms.
Secretary of State Marco Rubio says President Trump has not decided on any new strikes.
The distinction is the story. This is a standing instruction, not a launched operation. It changes what a strike would cost without deciding whether one happens.
The timing is the other variable. Mediation is aimed squarely at the window after Tuesday's midterms.
For markets, the transmission is conditional. An unrestricted-response doctrine lifts the risk premium in crude and in Middle East shipping — and in the insurance that covers both.
Crude carries war risk; shipping carries it more directly, because the war-risk component sits inside the freight rate.
No oil price move is supplied to confirm that repricing, so confirm nothing. Tanker rates and war-risk premia are where it would show up first.
The next observable test is the mediation itself. If it produces a framework, the premium deflates. If it fails, the post-midterm window reopens with the doctrine already in place.
So: an unrestricted response ordered, twelve hours of mediation against it, and a decision on new strikes still unmade.
