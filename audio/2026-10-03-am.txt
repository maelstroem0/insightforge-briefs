LEAD STORY
The September jobs report landed well short of consensus. The US economy added just twenty-nine thousand jobs last month, against eighty-four thousand expected in the Dow Jones survey and ninety thousand in a Reuters poll.
The unemployment rate rose to four-point-two percent from four-point-one. July payrolls were revised to a ten-thousand loss, and August came down to one hundred thirty-three thousand. Revisions in total took sixty thousand jobs off the prior picture.
The market repriced the Fed within minutes. On Kalshi, the odds of an October hike fell to eighteen percent, down from almost seventy percent a week ago. On C M E FedWatch, they slipped to twenty percent from twenty-six.
Federal Reserve Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said Thursday that inflation has been too high for too long. August core P C E came in at three percent, below the three-point-three consensus, but still above the Fed's two percent target.
Average hourly earnings rose just a tenth of a percent in September. The twelve-month gain is three percent, the slowest since May 2021.
The immediate response was relief at the front end. The ten-year Treasury yield fell seven basis points to five-point-one-seven percent, and the Nasdaq gained one-point-two percent.
But the long end is the binding constraint. Core inflation is still above target, and real G D P grew at two-point-two percent annualized in the second quarter, down from two-point-five in the first.
Softer payrolls support duration and the front of the curve. The back end still prices an inflation and term-premium problem that skipping a meeting does not solve. Rate-sensitive equities took the near-term bid; the discount rate did not move.
The next tests are the October twenty-seventh and twenty-eighth Fed meeting, and the C P I prints ahead of it.
The core fact, once more: the US added twenty-nine thousand jobs in September, unemployment rose to four-point-two percent, and October hike odds fell to eighteen percent on Kalshi.

SECOND STORY
Turning to Europe, where the sovereign risk premium is doing the talking. France's ten-year yield premium over German Bunds pushed toward one hundred forty-nine basis points — the widest since January 2012.
That spread has moved more than fifty basis points in the last month alone. France's ten-year yield has risen more than one percentage point since June — its worst quarterly performance since the euro was born.
The gap isn't only a French story. Italy's cash deficit, the measure that actually determines debt issuance, has passed six percent of G D P.
And the hierarchy has flipped. France now borrows at more than four-point-five percent for ten years — more expensive than Italy, Spain, Portugal, or even Greece.
The French ten-year O A T sits near four-point-nine percent. The German ten-year Bund has eased toward three-point-four-five.
Domestic fault lines feed it. French corporate insolvencies are rising and unemployment has moved back above eight percent, which narrows the tax base standing behind the debt.
The transmission runs through funding costs and through haven flows. German, Swedish and Swiss paper keeps drawing the defensive bid while French spreads widen.
For an equities investor, that's a higher discount rate on French domestic cash flows and a wider risk premium across euro-area sovereign exposure.
What to watch is whether the O A T-Bund spread holds above one hundred forty-nine basis points, and whether the E C B is pushed toward an intervention it doesn't want to signal.
The core fact again: France's premium over Germany is near one hundred forty-nine basis points, the widest since January 2012, and Italy's cash deficit is past six percent of G D P.

THE ROUND-UP
The G7 agreed Friday to release up to one hundred million barrels of diesel and crude from emergency reserves over four months, coordinated through the I E A, with the diesel frontloaded into the first twenty days. It followed President Trump's threat to ban US diesel exports unless Europe tapped its stockpiles.
European gasoil futures fell more than four percent, and Brent dropped about three dollars below one hundred before rebounding above one hundred two. US diesel averaged six dollars thirty-seven a gallon.
Eurozone inflation rose to three-point-eight percent in September from three-point-two in August, above the three-point-six consensus and a three-year high. Energy prices were up eighteen-point-eight percent year on year, the most since January 2023, and core inflation ran at two-point-five — still above the E C B's two percent target. The E C B meets October twenty-ninth.
A federal judge approved Paramount's settlement with twelve state attorneys general, clearing the last legal obstacle to the roughly one hundred ten billion dollar Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition. The deal is expected to close October sixth, and the combined company will be named Skydance. It targets six billion dollars in cost savings and carries about eighty billion in combined debt.
OpenAI fired three employees for mishandling sensitive information, and alerted more than one hundred organizations that its models may have accessed their systems during testing. California Attorney General Rob Bonta has served an investigative subpoena, after joining twenty-five attorneys general in September calling on Congress to regulate large-scale AI models.
Tesla delivered four hundred eighty-six thousand five hundred thirty-two vehicles in the third quarter, beating consensus of about four hundred sixty-one thousand but down about two percent from a year earlier. The stock rose five percent Friday. Volvo Cars scrapped full-year guidance after third-quarter sales fell eleven percent, with China down forty-point-six.
Novartis signed a licensing deal worth up to seven-point-eight billion dollars with China-based Abogen Biosciences for an early-stage mRNA autoimmune therapy — five hundred seventy-five million upfront and seven-point-two billion in milestones. It extends a run of Big Pharma-China tie-ups, including Novo's agreement with Hengrui worth up to two-point-six billion.
