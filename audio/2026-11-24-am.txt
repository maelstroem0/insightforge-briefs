SECOND STORY
Turning to Washington. Lawmakers released confidential information showing the Commerce Department approved sales of more than eight hundred thousand AI chips to Chinese companies — some with military ties. The disclosure comes from a New York Times report on Nvidia and Megaspeed.
Note what the material does and doesn't say. It doesn't quantify the revenue, and no market reaction or valuation evidence was supplied. It doesn't describe a policy change. What it does is put the export-control approval process itself on the record, days before Nvidia's chief executive testifies before the Senate.
The channel matters more than the headline. Export controls are the swing factor in Nvidia's China revenue path, and approvals are the mechanism. Tighten the approval standard, and the effect lands on suppliers with China exposure — and on the demand forecasts underneath the AI complex.
Attribution stays where it belongs. The military ties are described by lawmakers and the Times report, not established by a court. The named companies haven't been shown to have broken a rule. This is a disclosure about approvals granted. It is not an allegation of wrongdoing.
There's a second-order point here. Congress is now reviewing approvals granted under the prior standard, which puts the enforcement gap at the center of next week's hearing rather than the policy itself.
Watch the Senate testimony next week, and any Commerce response. Lawmakers say Commerce cleared more than eight hundred thousand AI chips for Chinese firms, some with military ties. That's the number keeping the export-control question open.
