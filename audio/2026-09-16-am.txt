LEAD STORY
India's stock market fell back below five trillion dollars on Tuesday. The combined market value of companies listed on the B S E dropped to four-point-nine-two trillion dollars, a level last seen on June eleventh. The Sensex fell seven hundred seventy-eight points, about one percent. And India's share of global market capitalization slipped to around two-point-nine-five percent, its lowest since June third.
The fuse was lit outside India. The ten-year US Treasury yield touched five-point-zero-four-one percent, its highest since 2007. Brent crude pushed above one hundred eight dollars a barrel after the shutdown of Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline. Higher US yields and a higher barrel don't stay offshore for long.
For an equity investor, that's an imported inflation shock and a foreign-flow problem at the same time. India buys most of its crude abroad, so a higher barrel feeds straight into the current account. A five percent US ten-year raises the return on the alternative, and that pulls money out of emerging-market equities. Long-duration Indian technology and industrial names carry the most rate sensitivity. The rupee carries the rest.
Tuesday's move was broad rather than selective. The Nifty fifty fell one-point-two percent. The drop in market capitalization matters because it reverses a milestone India crossed only recently.
What to watch: foreign portfolio flow data, and whether the Reserve Bank of India shifts its language at the next policy meeting. If the ten-year holds above five percent and Brent holds above one-oh-eight, the pressure on Indian equities doesn't have an obvious domestic fix. The core fact again: India's market value is back below five trillion dollars, and the trigger came from the US yield and the oil price, not from Delhi.

SECOND STORY
Turning to Washington. The Senate voted forty-nine to fifty against advancing the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, falling short of the sixty votes needed to move it forward.
The bill would have written a federal rulebook for digital assets. It failed on unresolved ethics concerns and banking-industry opposition to the stablecoin rewards language. So the largest crypto market in the world still has no legislative framework — only agency rulemaking.
The market reaction was immediate and specific. X R P dropped nearly ten percent to one dollar thirty as of Asian morning hours Wednesday, the worst performer among the majors, according to CoinDesk data. Bitcoin slipped nearly three percent to just above seventy-six thousand. Coinbase fell nearly nine percent to one seventy-four forty-two. Circle dropped more than nine percent to eighty-eight twenty-six.
That's the pattern worth noting. The tokens and the listed equities moved together, and the equity moves were larger in percentage terms than bitcoin's. Crypto-linked companies are now a levered expression of US regulatory risk.
What this leaves in place is the S E C and the C F T C, working through rulemaking rather than statute. That's a slower path, and it's reversible with a change of administration. It also opens a door for Europe. The EU's MiCA framework is already live, and a stalled US bill gives European regulators a recruiting tool for listings and staff.
Distribution channels are the thing to watch from here. Coinbase and Circle both earn from a regulated US market that doesn't yet exist in law. Watch whether the S E C advances stablecoin or custody rules on its own, and whether any issuer shifts primary operations toward Europe. The bottom line: the Clarity Act failed forty-nine to fifty, and US digital-asset regulation stays with the agencies.

THE OUTLIER
One oddity to close on. Hapag-Lloyd and Maersk's Gemini alliance is expanding Suez routings despite the Houthi threat. Sea Intelligence forecasts more than a quarter of Asia-Europe capacity transiting the Red Sea in September, and roughly thirty-five percent of Asia-Mediterranean headhaul capacity returning through Suez.
The transmission is freight economics. Asia-North Europe spot rates fell three percent last week to about four thousand three hundred dollars per forty-foot box, and daily pricing on both lanes has eased to around three thousand eight hundred this week. Cheaper freight lowers landed costs for European importers. The watchpoint is whether rates keep easing or the diversions return.
