LEAD STORY
The U.S. gross national debt passed forty trillion dollars on Thursday. It has doubled since January 2017. Debt held by the public is about thirty-two trillion dollars, and the federal government is projected to spend more than one trillion dollars on net interest this year. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says, 'There's nothing magic about the forty trillion dollar number.' The bond market is less relaxed. The ten-year Treasury is around four-point-seven percent, and the thirty-year is near five. To slow the move, the Treasury said it would at least double buybacks of longer-dated debt, with operations starting September ninth and potentially exceeding four billion dollars per issue. The relief lasted hours. Yields rebounded, the Dow fell seven hundred three points, the S and P five hundred lost almost one percent, and the Nasdaq dropped one percent. The dollar slipped to a three-month low. Gold pushed above forty-five hundred dollars an ounce. The thirty-year bond is already at its highest level in almost two decades. Investors are reading the buyback as a sign of fiscal stress, not a cure for it. If U.S. debt is priced as a real credit risk, borrowing costs rise for expansion, hiring, and investment. That pressures equity valuations and widens credit spreads. Rate-sensitive growth and consumer shares led the decline, while energy names held up on oil. Bessent says the deficit has likely peaked and the plan is to grow out of it. The market wants proof. Bessent's buyback plan is intended to support liquidity, but it does not reduce the underlying supply of new debt. That is why the dollar is weakening even as yields rise. The Treasury can shift the maturity mix, but it cannot change the arithmetic. Again: forty trillion dollars in federal debt, and the bond market is not reassured. The next test is whether the ten-year holds below five percent into the September refunding.

SECOND STORY
Turning to energy and the Middle East. The Strait of Hormuz is nearly closed to commercial energy traffic. Only seven commodity vessels moved through the waterway on Thursday. That is half the previous day's count. Shipping trackers counted four ships entering and three leaving. None were very large crude carriers or L N G tankers. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the United States will impose 'the toughest sanctions in history' on Iran. Brent crude touched a four-week high just under ninety-five dollars a barrel. U.S. crude settled around ninety-four dollars. Gasoline is above four ten a gallon, and diesel is above five fifty. Before the war began in February, Hormuz carried nearly a fifth of global crude and L N G. Now that artery is effectively shut. The United Arab Emirates has suspended all trade with Iran after accusing Tehran of launching two missiles. The administration is framing this as economic warfare, not just sanctions. Insurance and freight costs are rising. War-risk premiums are climbing. The pressure is not limited to the Gulf. Somali pirates have hijacked a weapons cargo vessel off Somalia, and armed men boarded a tanker off Yemen and diverted it toward Somalia. That is two hijackings in four days. The risk is a multi-front supply shock. The next milestone is whether a single very large crude carrier attempts the transit. So far, the answer is no. That keeps a floor under crude. A fifth of global oil supply is waiting for that answer. Watch for any sign of tanker traffic resuming. If it does not, Brent above one hundred dollars becomes the base case. The core fact: the world's most important oil chokepoint is down to a handful of ships.

THE OUTLIER
Now for the outlier. More than one hundred fifty Polymarket wallets posted a ninety-seven percent win rate on war-related bets. Those wallets made eight million dollars. Researchers say that kind of hit rate is essentially impossible without an informational edge, and it raises concerns that some traders used classified military information. No charges have been filed. The pattern is almost too clean. The trades tracked events in the Iran conflict. Separately, corporate spending on U.S. House and Senate races reached five hundred seventeen million dollars in the fifteen months through the first quarter. That already exceeds the four hundred sixty-one million spent over the full 2024 cycle. Crypto, AI, and betting firms are the biggest new contributors. The top contributors are not traditional banks or manufacturers. They are platforms built on risk and prediction. It is a small number in dollar terms, but a large one for credibility. The outlier is not a market move. It is a question about information.
