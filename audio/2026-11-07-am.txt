LEAD STORY
US employers added a hundred sixty-five thousand jobs in October. The Dow Jones estimate was fifty-nine thousand. The Bureau of Labor Statistics published the figures, and unemployment fell to four-point-one percent against a four-point-two percent forecast.
Average hourly earnings rose four tenths of a percent on the month, the biggest gain since January. They were up three-point-one percent from a year earlier. August and September were revised up by a combined eighty-four thousand, and December hike odds on the C M E FedWatch measure sat near seventy-seven percent.
Those are the published numbers, and they land on a market already leaning hawkish. The read-across is the front end. Stronger hiring and lower unemployment support the case for a December move, and that pressure shows up first in two-year yields and the dollar.
Rate-sensitive equities carry the other end of it. Small caps and long-duration growth names are valued against the front end, so a hawkish repricing is a valuation event for them before it is an earnings event.
There's a wage story underneath. Average hourly earnings are running at three-point-one percent, while September C P I was three-point-seven percent. Prices are still outrunning paychecks, and that's the squeeze the Fed is watching.
The composition is worth one line. Prime-age participation held at eighty-three-point-seven percent, the highest since May. The federal government has shed eleven percent of its workforce, or three hundred twenty-eight thousand jobs, since January 2025.
The revision matters as much as the headline. Eighty-four thousand jobs that weren't in the last count are in this one, and that changes the three-month trend, not just the single print. Watch two dates: the December FOMC, and the C P I prints that get us there.
To restate it. A hundred sixty-five thousand jobs against fifty-nine thousand expected, unemployment at four-point-one, and a December hike now the base case in futures. One soft core inflation reading reopens the whole debate.
