LEAD STORY
President Trump is threatening to ban Bombardier from selling aircraft in the United States unless the Canadian planemaker builds them locally. The demand landed on Truth Social. Trump wrote, quote, N O M O R E SELLING BOMBARDIER IN The U N I T E D S T A T E S.
This is the sharpest escalation yet in the US-Canada trade war, now in its second week after Carney pulled out of talks and polls showed most Americans oppose the tariffs. But there's no formal rule yet. No tariff schedule, no timeline, no Commerce Department action — just a social-media ultimatum.
Bombardier is a bellwether for cross-border aerospace. Its US customer base, its supplier network and its financing all run through North American integration, so a market-access threat raises localized-production cost risk for the company and its suppliers on both sides of the border. Whether it becomes binding policy is a separate question — and today, it is a threat, not a rule.
The watch item is formalization. Any Commerce or U S T R action turns a post into a regulation, and that's when aerospace supply chains reprice. Until then, the headline carries risk; it does not yet carry force. Bombardier's US market access is the story — and its survival depends on what Washington does next.

SECOND STORY
Turning to London. Britain will ban trade in goods and services from Israel's illegal settlements. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced the ban, citing settlement expansion and settler violence as threats to a two-state solution.
The scale is modest in absolute terms. Total UK-Israel trade in goods and services was six billion pounds in 2025, and how much of it is disrupted depends on Israeli reprisals. But the diplomatic footprint is larger than the trade figure.
The move risks friction with Washington and with Jerusalem. It also follows Monday's joint condemnation by eight nations of Israeli ministers' remarks on Gaza displacement, so London is not acting alone in tone — only in instrument. A trade ban is a harder tool than a statement, and it invites a harder response.
For markets, the consequence is conditional. Israel-linked trade and UK-US relations carry a geopolitical-risk premium only if the ban passes and only if retaliation follows. The tests are legislative passage, Israeli countermeasures, and whether Washington chooses to answer an ally's trade policy with measures of its own.

THE OUTLIER
A Belgian arrest is feeding Europe's chip standoff. Belgian prosecutors say they detained a man in May as he was leaving for Beijing, suspected of siphoning chip-technology secrets to a Chinese company. The case, now public per the Wall Street Journal, hardens European fears of industrial espionage — and hardened fears become policy, in the form of export controls and technology-transfer rules that hit European chip-equipment names with China revenue. The watchpoint: the next prosecution, and any European Commission rules that follow it.
