He warned that rising debt costs amount to a progressive strangulation of the public finances. The French ten-year yield is trading around four-point-five percent. Both figures are levels reported this weekend, not a live print.
France still has no adopted budget, so there is no credible consolidation path on the table. The central bank forecasts zero-point-four percent growth this year, and zero-point-nine percent in 2027. That's the mechanism.
Debt service climbs, fiscal space shrinks, and the growth needed to stabilize the ratio doesn't arrive. For investors, the pressure transmits first through French duration and the O A T-Bund spread. It then reaches French banks and utilities, which price their own wholesale funding off that sovereign curve.

A spread that holds here is a funding cost, not a headline. The core fact again: France's central bank governor wants an emergency budget, and the O A T spread over Germany is near one hundred basis points.

SECOND STORY
Turning to the Strait of Hormuz, where the diplomatic window has closed again. President Trump has rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the strait within seven days and restart nuclear talks.
Iran had offered that in exchange for sanctions relief and an end to the US naval blockade, and transmitted it through Qatar. Trump said the deal would not be acceptable. Tehran says it will not change its conditions.
The shipping data is where this gets concrete. Kpler data cited by Reuters shows a recent ten-day average of about eighteen transits a day. Some days have seen as few as nine vessels. The US and Israel launched air strikes on Iran on February twenty-eighth.
Before the war, roughly twenty percent of the world's oil and L N G passed through that water. The strait remains partially closed. Year to date, W T I crude is up nearly sixty-one percent. Brent is more than seventy-one percent higher.
That keeps tanker rates, refining margins and shipping insurance bid, with transits running at a fraction of normal traffic. The other side of that ledger is Asian importers and airlines, where fuel is the largest variable cost.
The week's oil selloff came as US and Iranian officials met at the United Nations. This rejection points the other way. Iran's conditions are unchanged, so the seven-day framework is off the table, not modified.
What to watch: Washington's formal response, or resumed strikes, and the daily transit count. The core fact again: Trump has rejected Iran's seven-day reopening plan, and Hormuz transits are still near eighteen a day.
