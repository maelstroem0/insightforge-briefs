LEAD STORY
Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to fifteen billion to sixteen billion dollars, against roughly seventeen billion expected. That gap cost the stock twenty-eight-point-six percent on Friday — its worst single session since nineteen seventy-four.
The guide is the news. The reaction is the market's answer on AI capital spending. Intel's miss landed as Amazon, Alphabet and chip names sold off on the same question: what all this spending actually returns.
That's the channel, and it's why a single company guide travels. A bellwether stops being a company problem when the buyers of compute are being asked to justify the bill. Intel supplies into servers, PCs and the long tail of the buildout.
When the guide comes in around a billion dollars light, suppliers and customers read it as a demand signal. So the selling ran through chip names and the hyperscalers funding them, not just through Intel.
There is a cushion. The thirteen largest AI-related companies held one-point-one-three trillion dollars in cash, equivalents and marketable securities at their latest filings. That's a balance-sheet argument. It isn't a return argument, and the market spent Friday separating the two.
For anyone holding semis or data-center exposure, the question shifts from order books to payback periods. Revenue guidance is now the swing variable, and it cuts in both directions. Watch Apple, A M D and Palantir results, plus the October jobs report.
Those are the next readings on whether capex is slowing or merely being repriced. The core fact stands: Intel guided roughly a billion dollars light, and the stock lost twenty-eight-point-six percent in a session. The AI trade is now a cash-flow trade.

SECOND STORY
Turning to the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four tankers struck mines in the waterway, and the Houthis claimed strikes on Saudi energy facilities.
December Brent settled at a hundred and seventeen dollars and sixty-four cents a barrel, up thirteen-point-six percent in October. That's a fourth consecutive monthly gain. European gas moved with it — T T F settled at a hundred and one euros and eighty-two cents per megawatt-hour, its first close above a hundred euros since twenty twenty-two.
The transmission runs through freight and refining before it reaches the pump. War-risk insurance and rerouting raise the cost of moving a barrel. Roughly a tenth of global refining capacity is offline, and diesel has done more of the work than crude.
The offset is policy. Strategic petroleum reserve loans and continuing diplomacy cap the upside, and Qatar has been mediating in Tehran. So the risk premium is pricing two things at once: an interruption, and the chance it gets avoided.
The clean read for portfolios is energy earnings rather than a directional call on oil. Refining margins are the visible channel. The inflation read is narrower — gas and diesel feed European industrial costs and headline prints faster than they feed core.
Watch whether Hormuz flows are verifiably restored, or whether the talks produce another temporary reopening. Mines in a waterway are a physical fact. Brent at a hundred and seventeen sixty-four is the market's price for the disruption and for the diplomacy.
