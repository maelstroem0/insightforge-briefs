LEAD STORY
Wholesale inflation accelerated to a three-year high in October.
The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported producer prices up three tenths of a percent on the month, below the four tenths consensus. But the twelve-month rate rose to six-point-five percent — the steepest annual gain since November 2022.
Core prices, stripping out food, energy and trade services, rose three tenths on the month and five percent on the year. Intermediate processed goods are up fourteen-point-six percent from a year ago, the biggest such gain since July 2022.
It's broad, not a single-category distortion. Final demand goods are up ten-point-two percent over the year. The transportation and warehousing index is up fourteen percent, the worst since October 2022. Food prices for producers jumped one-point-three-four percent in a single month.
Note the nuance. The monthly figure came in under forecast, and so did the annual one. Neither miss was large enough to change the pricing that followed.
Traders read the print and moved. A December Fed rate rise is now priced at one hundred percent odds, and the ten-year Treasury yield touched five-point-one-seven-four percent intraday.
Here's the consequence. When wholesale inflation runs above six percent, the front end of the curve stays pinned and the discount rate on every long-duration asset stays high. Rate-sensitive equity multiples carry that cost first — utilities, REITs, anything whose cash flows sit years out.
And it isn't confined to producers. The consumer price index accelerated to four percent year over year in October. Businesses are absorbing more than households are, but the gap is narrowing from both directions.
The next observable test is November's C P I print, then the December FOMC. Watch whether the front end holds at these levels, or whether the long end takes the move instead.
So the number to carry: producer prices up six-point-five percent on the year, the fastest since November 2022, with a December hike now fully priced.

SECOND STORY
Turning to geopolitics. The White House says President Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin will hold the first-ever trilateral leaders' meeting, on the sidelines of the A P E C summit in Shenzhen on Thursday.
The stated agenda covers nuclear arms reduction and the Iran war. Ukraine is not on it.
Officials say they expect no nuclear agreement and describe the talks as a starting point. Set that as the base case honestly, because it defines what would actually count as a surprise on Thursday.
This builds on September, when Washington and Beijing agreed a thirty-billion-dollar tariff cut arrangement and opened an AI dialogue. That was a bilateral channel. This one puts three capitals at one table.
The transmission is risk premia, not earnings. A joint statement on nuclear limits or on Iran would lower defence and energy risk premia across European and Gulf-linked assets. A meeting that ends in photographs and no document leaves those premia exactly where they sit.
For equity investors, the sensitive exposures are European defence names and anything levered to Gulf crude flows and freight. Neither has a resolved outcome this morning, and neither should be traded on the headline alone.
There's a sequencing wrinkle worth naming. The bond market has already tightened financial conditions this week on its own. A geopolitical de-escalation headline would arrive into a market that has other reasons to sell.
The fact to hold: Trump, Xi and Putin, in Shenzhen on Thursday — the first trilateral of its kind, with nuclear arms and Iran on the table and Ukraine deliberately off it. Watch for a written commitment, or the absence of one.

MARKETS CHECK
Let's get to the markets, with Friday's close as the reference. Bitcoin led the damage, down about eleven and a half percent on the week — its sixth straight weekly decline.
Treasuries took the other side of the same trade. The two-year yield rose about forty basis points, the ten-year about thirty, and the thirty-year about twenty-two.
The dollar was firmer, up about one percent on the index. Gold fell four and a half percent. Brent slipped two and a half.
Equities, oddly, held. The S and P 500 finished the week up about two and a half percent, the Nasdaq 100 up three and a half, and Europe's benchmark up three and a half as well.

THE ROUND-UP
US Treasuries posted their worst week since Trump's liberation day tariff shock, as hotter inflation and hawkish Fed remarks stung investors who had bet the global bond rout was over. Duration is the transmission channel — rate-sensitive equities, REITs and emerging-market local debt carry the cost, and the reporting supplies no auction or flow data to soften that.
Bitcoin fell below seventy thousand dollars for the first time since August, touching sixty-seven thousand seven hundred seventy on Thursday, as liquidations topped one-point-six billion dollars in twenty-four hours, with longs taking nearly eighty-nine percent of the losses. US spot bitcoin ETFs logged a sixth straight outflow session, and open interest fell four and a half percent even as volume climbed.
Mercedes-Benz froze all new investment in German sites and warned it may close plants, with H R director Britta Seeger saying the company must examine which locations it can still afford. It's targeting a twenty percent cut in German labour costs, I G Metall has called twenty-four-hour warning strikes, and the freeze transmits straight into supplier order books and tooling capex.
In Washington, Senator Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic leader, with Schumer claiming public support from fourteen senators including himself, against five for Van Hollen. Twenty-eight or twenty-nine votes wins, the secret ballot is December first, and a change would shift Democratic positioning on Iran war funding, tariffs and a stock-trading ban.
The Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than one-point-two billion dollars of crypto stolen from Binance in an October twenty-ninth attack that took over six billion, attributed to North Korea-linked TraderTraitor. Authorities also seized infrastructure tied to FaceStealer, an impersonation scam that pulled in more than four hundred million dollars over eighteen months.
Underneath all five sits the same variable: the price of money, and the dollar that follows it.

THE OUTLIER
One structural item to close on. Berlin plans to buy the one-third stake in Urenco held by R W E and E.ON, for up to four billion euros, and the 2027 draft budget sets aside exactly that for energy-sector stakes. Britain asked Germany to buy it, to keep enrichment capacity in European hands.
Enrichment is the chokepoint in the nuclear fuel chain. Moving it from utility balance sheet to sovereign policy changes who absorbs a supply shock. Watch the final budget line. E.ON rose one-point-five percent Friday, R W E three tenths.
