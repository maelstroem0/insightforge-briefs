LEAD STORY
The global oil benchmark has given back roughly twenty dollars a barrel since late October, when it traded around one hundred thirteen. That's the Iran-war risk premium, unwinding in under two weeks.
On Monday, one report cited a further one percent dip as peace hopes firmed and supplies improved. A N Z says investor focus has turned to progress in the Iran war since Democrats' sweeping midterm election win — the bank's read, not a market print.
The transmission channel is straightforward. Crude is an input cost in everything from jet fuel to fertilizer to plastics. If the move holds, it eases global energy cost pressure and inflation expectations, with read-through to energy equities, transport costs and commodity-linked currencies.
It also changes the relative appeal of energy equities, which have been priced for a war markets now think is winding down. Two cautions, though. This is a de-escalation hope trade, not a signed deal. The word is hopes. Nothing has been agreed.
And no consensus forecast for where the benchmark settles was supplied — so the earnings transmission for producers and refiners can't be sized from what we have. What we do have is direction. Twenty dollars down from a hundred thirteen is a material repricing of geopolitical risk, and it happened in roughly ten sessions.
For a global investor, that's the arithmetic: cheaper crude is disinflationary at the margin, and it takes risk premium out of everything downstream of the barrel — airlines, chemicals, staples, emerging-market importers.
Watch whether talks produce something with a signature on it. If they don't, the premium comes back, and it comes back fast — the physical supply picture hasn't changed. For now, the global oil benchmark is down roughly twenty dollars a barrel since late October, from around one hundred thirteen.

SECOND STORY
Turning to crypto. Bitcoin closed below its fifty-week moving average for the first time in eight weeks. The level is seventy-five thousand four hundred eighty-eight dollars.
Galaxy's head of firmwide research, Alex Thorn, notes bitcoin finished the week about seven-tenths of a percent below that average, and briefly traded under seventy-four thousand.
The deleveraging was violent. Total crypto liquidations exceeded one point one five billion dollars in twenty-four hours, and one point zero four billion of that was longs. The single largest liquidation was an ether position worth forty-eight million dollars.
The institutional flow tells the same story. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded one point two eight billion dollars of net outflows last week, their largest weekly withdrawals since late June. BlackRock's I B I T alone shed four hundred three million dollars on Friday.
At the time of publication bitcoin traded near seventy-four thousand and forty-nine dollars — down roughly five percent over seven days and two point three percent in twenty-four hours. Galaxy's previous cycle low was fifty-nine thousand five hundred seventeen dollars on June twenty-eighth.
The firm argued last month that the bear market was over. Now contrast the haven trade. Gold held firm above four thousand dollars an ounce and traded near four thousand one hundred, with silver around fifty-two fifty.
That's the tell: capital isn't leaving risk, it's choosing which store of value it trusts. The Bitcoin Fear and Greed Index printed twenty-nine on Monday — fear, not capitulation.
Watch whether bitcoin holds the seventy-four thousand four hundred to seventy-five thousand zone, and whether E T F redemptions slow. US inflation data is the next catalyst.

THE ROUND-UP
Elsewhere, Nvidia shares fell more than eight percent in opening trading Monday. The chipmaker said its board launched an investigation of C E O Jensen Huang, following reports he was targeted by Chinese intelligence.
Nvidia and Huang were also sued in a proposed class action accusing them of defrauding shareholders by concealing his ties with a suspected Chinese spy. The probe's scope and findings are the next observable test, and no guidance impact has been supplied.
The Senate voted seventy-three to twenty-five to advance the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act — a four hundred seventeen page bill that would speed federal permitting for power plants, transmission lines, gas pipelines and data centers.
It would also require data-center operators to pay for grid upgrades. T D Cowen cut its estimated passage probability to twenty-five percent from forty. For scale: at the end of 2025, two thousand and sixty gigawatts of generation and storage sat in the interconnection queue.
Coinbase won final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to charter Coinbase National Trust Company, moving its crypto custody business under federal bank supervision. The sign-off came seven months after preliminary conditional approval on April second.
The O C C has processed nearly two dozen such charters in the second Trump administration, more than a dozen of them to crypto firms — while traditional banks sue over the framework.
China's Ministry of Transport extended its suspension of special port fees on US ships, and the related investigation, until January tenth, 2027. Its Commerce Ministry extended export-control suspensions covering sixteen US entities and unreliable-entity-list measures on eleven US firms for two months. A twenty-four percent tariff on US goods was extended too.
Honda will spend three billion dollars on a new Ohio assembly plant, starting production in 2029, with capacity for two hundred forty thousand vehicles a year and more than two thousand jobs.
That lifts Honda's US light-duty vehicle capacity to one point three million units annually — a hybrid bet, after it suspended its Canada E V plan.
And ExxonMobil signed a memorandum of understanding with Caracas to explore drilling opportunities in Venezuela, nearly two decades after leaving the country. Its C E O called Venezuela uninvestable in January. No volumes, capital spending, acreage, partner or fiscal terms were disclosed.

THE OUTLIER
One more, and it's a signal rather than a price. Axios reports oil prices and bond yields have stopped moving together — breaking the relationship that has anchored cross-asset hedging through the Iran-war supply shock.
No levels, no correlation readings and no measurement window were supplied, so this can't be sized. But the transmission is plain: if energy stops hedging duration and inflation risk, portfolios built on that pairing need new offsets. Watch the next inflation print, and whether a Hormuz escalation re-couples them.
