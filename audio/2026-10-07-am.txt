Yields don't queue — they price. I'm Iris, and this is The ApexQuantix Briefings. It's Wednesday, October seventh. France's ten-year now yields more than Greece's — and Brent is back above a hundred dollars as the E I A lifts its forecast. Plus, stocks bid, the dollar firm, crude choppy.

LEAD STORY
France's ten-year government bond now yields more than Greece's. It also yields more than Italy's. U B S chief executive Sergio Ermotti says France needs hard measures — not incremental changes — to deal with its debt pile.
The benchmark French ten-year stood at four-point-seven-six-eight-nine percent Tuesday afternoon, after easing nine basis points. The spread over German Bunds is above one hundred forty basis points. That spread is the number that matters.
It touched one hundred forty-nine basis points earlier this month, the widest since January 2012. It hasn't returned to what anyone would call comfortable.
The stress has already moved into the banks. French bank credit spreads exceed the European peer average by fifteen to twenty basis points.
The index of European bank AT1 subordinated bonds has lost two percent in total return since August. Banks fund themselves against that sovereign curve, and they pay for it.
From there the cost travels — into mortgage pricing, corporate lending, and eventually the real economy. That is the transmission, and it is already open.
Why it matters beyond France: the euro area's second-largest economy is now the one paying a peripheral yield. That sets the funding benchmark for French corporates and for the banks that lend to them.
Investors are watching the fiscal position and the political calendar at once. The next test is whether the spread holds above one-forty into the spring presidential election.
A poorly received auction or syndication would confirm the repricing. So would another leg wider in French bank credit.
For now, the fact is simple. France's ten-year yields more than Greece's — and U B S says the remedy has to be structural, not cosmetic.

SECOND STORY
Turning to energy. Brent crude is back above one hundred dollars a barrel — and Washington just raised its forecast for next year.
Brent futures gained ninety-three cents, or about nine-tenths of a percent, to one hundred one dollars and fifty-one cents. West Texas Intermediate rose about one percent to ninety dollars and thirty cents.
Two things are driving it. A storm forming in the Gulf of Mexico threatens roughly fifteen percent of US crude output, and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated.
The US Energy Information Administration now expects Brent to average about ninety-eight dollars a barrel in 2026 — an eight percent increase from its prior projection.
The head of the trading house Vitol says around twelve million barrels a day of crude and two million barrels a day of refined products left the Middle East on tankers over the past seven to ten days.
US crude stockpiles fell two-point-oh-nine million barrels in the week ended October second.
For an equities investor, a sustained hundred-dollar barrel feeds headline inflation, squeezes transport and consumer discretionary margins, and supports energy earnings.
The refined side is where it bites hardest. US retail diesel prices hit records last month and are projected to stay above six dollars a gallon in October, before averaging about four-fifty in 2027.
The E I A expects crude-production shutdowns to fall from four-point-five million barrels a day in the fourth quarter of 2026 to two-point-seven million in the first quarter of 2027.
Whether that relief reaches the pump is the number to watch. For now, the barrel is still the beta — Brent above a hundred.

THE ROUND-UP
Germany's factory orders plunged ten-point-six percent in August, against expectations of a one percent decline. Aircraft, ship, train and military vehicle orders slumped almost sixty-two percent after surging nearly a hundred thirty percent in July. Excluding big-ticket orders, demand slipped just one tenth of a percent.
The Peterson Institute for International Economics projects China's economy will grow four-point-six percent this year and four-point-three percent in 2027, down from five percent in 2025. Capital Economics separately puts the yuan as much as twenty-five percent undervalued.
Google and Constellation Energy signed a twenty-year power purchase agreement for three thousand five hundred ninety megawatts. That includes eight hundred ninety megawatts of new nuclear capacity from uprates at eleven plants across Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey.
Constellation expects to spend four-point-three billion dollars, with the first uprated reactor targeted for 2028.
New York Fed research finds a ten percent tariff-driven rise in import and producer prices passes through as five-point-six percent higher consumer retail prices. The same work forecasts that price-level effect easing to about two percent by August 2026.
Shipping crude from the Persian Gulf to China now costs one million dollars a day per tanker. Nearly twenty commercial ships, mostly tankers, have been attacked in the past month, and Hormuz flows averaged about ten-point-three million barrels a day in the week ended Saturday — roughly twenty-three percent below the prewar baseline.
Wall Street's broker-dealer profits hit forty-five-point-nine billion dollars in the first half, fifty-one-point-three percent above last year and already past the full-year 2026 forecast. Underwriting fees rose sixty-eight percent, with global equity issuance up seventy-six-point-five percent.
The US trade deficit widened to one hundred five-point-six billion dollars in August, up thirteen-point-seven percent from July and the widest since March 2025. Imports rose four-point-three percent on AI-related goods, and Goldman Sachs cut its third-quarter G D P tracking estimate to three-point-one percent.

THE OUTLIER
One more before we go. Goldman Sachs now forecasts global diesel and jet-fuel crack spreads will average above forty dollars a barrel in 2027 — more than twice their usual level near twenty. The reason is refining, not crude: roughly two million barrels a day of Middle Eastern refining capacity remains offline. Emergency crude doesn't replace a refinery. Watch restart schedules and weekly inventory data.
