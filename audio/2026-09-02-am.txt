Iran answered with twenty-five ballistic missiles at US bases in Jordan. Half reached Jordanian airspace, ten were intercepted, three landed without casualties. Two Filipino seafarers died Monday on a Saudi tanker. Iran's military command warned of, quote, heavier, more widespread, and devastating responses.
President Donald Trump escalated on Truth Social. He said, quote, if the failed Nation of Iran retaliates for this very justified attack, they will be hit again at a much harder and higher level. He added that almost total control of the Hormuz Strait puts him in a much better position.
Here is why this matters beyond the oil price. Only forty ships carried millions of barrels through the strait Tuesday, and Energy Secretary Chris Wright says seventeen million barrels moved Monday. If the chokepoint stays this tight, energy inflation feeds directly into the inflation data the Fed is watching. Markets now price a September rate hike at roughly seventy percent. That's a stagflation shock — oil up, yields up, equities down, all at once.

And the transit count itself, every morning. Four ships versus thirteen is the number that proves this matters, and it's the number to check again tomorrow.

SECOND STORY
Turning to trade, where the tariff war with Canada just went from threat to schedule. After talks collapsed on August twenty-first, Washington levied fifty percent tariffs on twenty billion dollars of Canadian goods. Ottawa will retaliate dollar-for-dollar starting September eighth — same twenty billion dollar basket, at rates from fifteen to fifty percent.
Prime Minister Mark Carney set the tone in public. He said, quote, when the Americans stop doing memes, stop throwing shade, stop trying to be tough and start being serious about having those discussions, we can have those discussions. For its part, the US trade representative's office was blunt in the other direction. Jamieson Greer said, quote, there are no negotiations happening on trade right now.
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent framed the asymmetry this way. He said, quote, I don't think you can be in a tit-for-tat with someone who's thirteen times larger than you are. The arithmetic supports him — polls show fifty-seven percent of Americans oppose tariffs on Canada — but the White House is not backing off the schedule.
Bessent also used the moment to isolate Beijing. At the G20, nineteen of twenty members backed a statement against non-market export practices. Bessent said, quote, it is clear that the country with the world's largest and unsustainable current account surplus, the People's Republic of China, was the dissenter. China's surplus hit a record one-point-two trillion dollars in 2025.
The investor consequences are concrete. Bank of America expects the Bank of Canada to hold at two-and-a-quarter percent, with tariffs adding roughly three-tenths of a point to inflation and cutting the same from G D P. That's a rates divergence trade against a market still pricing cuts. Short Canadian-exposed industrials and the Canadian dollar is the clean expression.
The date to hold onto is September eighth — the retaliation clock is already running. And watch whether the Bank of Canada's language next week confirms what Bessent's G20 move signals: trade blocs are hardening, and pricing is following.
