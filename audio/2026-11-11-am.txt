LEAD STORY
October consumer prices came in hot, and the December hike trade is now the base case. Headline C P I rose four tenths of a percent on the month, against three tenths expected. Core rose three tenths — a third straight month at that pace, above the two tenths consensus.
Core prices ran at a three-point-seven percent annualized pace over the three months through October, the highest since January 2025. Supercore, core services excluding housing, accelerated to five-point-five percent annualized, the strongest since April 2024, from four-point-six percent in September.
Fed Governor Christopher Waller says a fifty-basis-point hike in December should be considered if core rose at least three tenths of a percent. It did. That turns a contingency into a live question for December.
The market didn't wait. The two-year Treasury yield rose to four-point-six-seven percent, and the ten-year spiked about twelve basis points to five percent. Hours later, the Treasury had to pay five-point-zero-two percent to sell forty-two billion dollars of ten-year notes.
Deutsche Bank now models twenty-five-basis-point hikes in December, January and March, taking the target range to four-point-five to four-point-seven-five percent. For an equities-led portfolio, that's a direct hit to the discount rate on long-duration growth, and support for the dollar.
There's relief in one corner. Diesel is six dollars fifteen a gallon, down about thirteen cents in a month, and the national average for regular gasoline slipped below four dollars for the first time since July.
So the core fact, restated: October core C P I rose three tenths of a percent for a third consecutive month, and the ten-year touched five percent. Watch the December FOMC — and whether the dot plot matches the hike Waller just put in play.

SECOND STORY
Turning to Taiwan. T S M C's October revenue rose fifty-nine-point-seven percent from a year earlier, to a record five hundred eighty-six-point-eight-nine billion Taiwan dollars — about eighteen-point-four-six billion US dollars at the company's guidance rate.
January-through-October revenue is up forty-three-point-three percent at four-point-four-nine trillion Taiwan dollars, tracking the roughly forty-four percent full-year growth T S M C has guided to. October's jump came on robust AI-related chip manufacturing demand.
The board went further than the top line. It raised the third-quarter dividend to a record nine Taiwan dollars a share, from seven. It approved forty-point-nine-three billion US dollars of capital appropriations.
It also approved up to five hundred million dollars each for an ecosystem investment company, for Anthropic common shares, and for OpenAI preferred stock.
Here's the wider channel. Appropriations of that size convert into orders for semiconductor equipment and materials — the part of the AI supply chain furthest from the model-builders and closest to the cycle.
Revenue is reported fact. The capex approval is a board decision. Neither tells you the return on it. The foundry's cash going into Anthropic and OpenAI equity also ties its balance sheet to model-builder risk.
The Anthropic and OpenAI stakes are capped at five hundred million dollars each. The capital appropriations are the larger number, and they're what the equipment chain reads.
So: record October revenue, up nearly sixty percent, and a forty-point-nine-three-billion-dollar capital budget. The next test is whether that spending shows up in equipment orders — and whether full-year growth holds near forty-four percent.
